Brown Butter Chocolate Chunk Cookies

2026-11-22 · Makes about 16 large cookies

Ingredients

  • 1¾ cups (8¾ oz) unbleached all-purpose flour
  • ½ tsp baking soda
  • 14 Tbsp (1¾ sticks) unsalted butter, divided: 10 Tbsp to brown, 4 Tbsp to stir in
  • 2 Tbsp (⅛ cup) nonfat dry milk powder
  • ¾ cup (5¼ oz) packed dark brown sugar
  • ½ cup (3½ oz) granulated sugar
  • 1 tsp table salt
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 3¾ oz dark chocolate bar, chopped
  • 3¾ oz semisweet or milk chocolate bar, chopped
  • ¾ cup chopped pecans or walnuts, toasted (optional)

Method

  1. Set a rack in the middle of the oven and heat it to 375°F. Line 2 large baking sheets with parchment.
  2. Whisk the flour and baking soda in a medium bowl.
  3. Melt 10 Tbsp of the butter in a 10-inch skillet over medium-high heat, about 2 minutes. Do not use nonstick. The dark coating hides the color of the butter. Whisk in the milk powder. Keep cooking, swirling the pan all the time, until the butter is dark golden and the solids are deep brown and smell nutty, 1 to 3 minutes. The milk powder browns faster than the butter alone, so watch closely. Scrape it all, browned bits included, into a large heatproof bowl. Stir in the remaining 4 Tbsp butter until it melts.
  4. Whisk in both sugars, the salt, and the vanilla. Add the egg and yolk and whisk until smooth with no sugar lumps, about 30 seconds. Let stand 3 minutes, then whisk 30 seconds. Rest and whisk two more times, until the mixture is thick, smooth, and shiny.
  5. Stir in the flour mixture with a rubber spatula just until combined, about 1 minute. Stir in the chocolate and nuts, if using, until no pockets of flour remain.
  6. Divide the dough into 16 portions, about 3 Tbsp each. Set them 2 inches apart on the baking sheets.
  7. Bake one sheet at a time, rotating it halfway, until the cookies are golden brown and still puffy, with set edges and soft centers, 10 to 14 minutes. Set the sheet on a wire rack and let the cookies cool on it to room temperature.

Notes

Adapted from America’s Test Kitchen’s Classic Chocolate Chip Cookies. Our changes: milk powder browned with the butter, and chopped bar chocolate, half dark and half semisweet or milk, in place of chips.

Why milk powder: the nutty flavor of brown butter comes from its milk solids, and butter has only a little of them. Milk powder adds more solids to toast, so the butter tastes deeper, like toffee and caramel. It also browns faster. Pull the pan at deep golden, because burnt solids taste bitter.

Why bar chocolate: chips have stabilizers that help them hold their shape. A chopped bar melts into pools, and the small shards streak through the dough. Dark chocolate cuts the sweetness, and the semisweet or milk keeps it from reading bitter.

Melted butter, the extra yolk, and more brown sugar than white all make the cookies chewy. The rest-and-whisk steps dissolve the sugar in the warm butter, which gives crisp edges and a toffee flavor. Use fresh, soft dark brown sugar. Hard brown sugar makes dry cookies. Light brown sugar works but tastes milder.

Take the cookies out while the centers still look underdone. They finish setting on the hot sheet. With smaller baking sheets, bake fewer cookies per sheet, in more batches.
