Walnut Tart Dough

2026-11-01 · Makes one 9-inch single crust

Ingredients

  • ½ cup walnuts
  • 1⅔ cups (7.25 oz) unbleached all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
  • ¼ cup plus 1 Tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 4 oz very cold unsalted butter, cut into 8 pieces
  • 2 Tbsp very cold water

Method

  1. Put the walnuts in a food processor. Pulse until they are very fine and even, but not so far that they turn to nut butter.
  2. Add the flour, sugar, and salt. Pulse a few times to blend.
  3. Add the butter. Pulse until the largest piece is the size of a small pea.
  4. With the processor running, drizzle in the water. Process until the mixture climbs the sides of the bowl. Take off the lid and squeeze a big pinch. If it is dry and crumbly, pulse in a few more drops of water. If it holds together and feels moist, stop.
  5. Tip the dough onto a lightly floured counter and gather it into a ball. Push it away from you with the heel of your hand, then scrape it back into a ball with a bench scraper. Repeat a few strokes, until the dough starts to come together. It is fine if it is still a little crumbly. Do not overwork it.
  6. Shape the dough into a flat disk. Wrap it in plastic and chill about 30 minutes.
  7. Before rolling, let the dough sit out until it bends without cracking. After a long chill, this takes 15 to 20 minutes.

Notes

Adapted from Joshua McFadden with Martha Holmberg, Six Seasons: A New Way with Vegetables (Artisan, 2017). The original is the Pecan Dough on p. 49, made here with walnuts. The book says any nut works, in the same amount.

The dough has a high butter-to-flour ratio, so it is rich, tender, and a little tricky to roll. Pea-size pieces of butter melt in the oven and leave flaky layers. The minimum of water keeps gluten low, so the crust does not toughen or shrink. This is why you do not knead pastry dough.

Yield: one batch makes one 9-inch single crust. The Shallot and Guanciale Tart takes two-thirds of a batch for a 10-inch pan. Wrap the last third and freeze it.

Storage: 2 days in the fridge, 3 months in the freezer. Thaw frozen dough overnight in the fridge.

Cold dough cracks. My first batch sat in the fridge overnight, and it cracked when I rolled it straight from the cold. The book says to let it sit at room temperature before rolling. Give it 15 to 20 minutes until it bends without splitting.

Work fast and keep your hands off it. Warm butter makes the dough soft and greasy. If the dough turns soft while you roll, put it back in the fridge for 10 minutes before you go on. Patch small cracks with scraps and a dab of water.
