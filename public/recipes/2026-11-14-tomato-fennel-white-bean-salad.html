Tomato, Fennel, and White Bean Salad with Torn Bread and Smoky Aioli

2026-11-14 · Serves 3 or 4

Ingredients

  • 2 egg yolks
  • 1 garlic clove, grated
  • 2 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 2 Tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • ½ tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • ½ cup neutral oil, such as vegetable, canola, or grapeseed
  • ½ cup extra-virgin olive oil, for the aioli
  • 8 oz mixed heirloom and cherry tomatoes, cherries halved and larger ones cut into chunks
  • 1½ cups thinly sliced fennel (from 1 medium bulb)
  • 1 small shallot, thinly sliced into rings
  • 1½ cups cooked large white beans, such as Royal Corona or Large White Lima, drained
  • 5 Tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, divided, plus more for drizzling
  • Kosher salt and black pepper
  • 6 oz rustic bread, torn into 1-inch pieces
  • 1½ tsp 'nduja
  • Small fistful of basil, torn
  • Flaky salt, for serving

Method

  1. Make the aioli. Put the egg yolks, garlic, mustard, lemon juice, and salt in the bottom of an immersion blender cup. Pour both oils over the top. Set the blender flat on the bottom of the cup and turn it on. It starts to emulsify almost at once. Slowly lift the blender up through the oil until everything is thick and smooth. Taste and add salt. You need ½ cup. The rest keeps, covered and refrigerated, for up to 1 week.
  2. In a large bowl, gently toss the tomatoes, fennel, shallot, beans, 2 Tbsp of the olive oil, a good pinch of kosher salt, and a few grinds of pepper. Set aside.
  3. Heat the remaining 3 Tbsp olive oil in a 12-inch skillet over medium heat until it shimmers. Add the bread and cook, stirring now and then, until toasty and golden, 5 to 7 minutes. Take it off the heat and let it cool a little.
  4. Meanwhile, mix ½ cup of the aioli with the 'nduja until smooth. Swoosh it across a large plate or platter with the back of a spoon.
  5. Toss the bread with the tomato and fennel mixture. The tomatoes will have given off enough juice to coat everything. Taste and add salt and pepper. Pile it on the aioli.
  6. Top with the basil, a drizzle of olive oil, and a few pinches of flaky salt.

Notes

Adapted from Colu Henry, Better at Home: Recipes for Big Nights In (Abrams, 2026), as printed in the Rancho Gordo Bean Club newsletter, 3rd Quarter 2026.

The 'nduja turns plain aioli smoky and a little spicy. A teaspoon and a half is enough to flavor half a cup. If you skip the homemade aioli, mayonnaise works.

The aioli works because the immersion blender starts in a small pool of yolk at the bottom of a narrow cup. The blade emulsifies a little oil at a time as you lift it. A wide bowl spreads the yolk too thin to catch. The mustard is there as a binder, not for flavor.

This salad works like a panzanella. The salted tomatoes give off juice, and the toasted bread drinks it up while the edges stay crisp. Toss and serve right away, before the bread goes soft. Big, creamy runner beans like Royal Corona hold their shape against the bread.
