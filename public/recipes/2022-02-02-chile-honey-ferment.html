Chile Honey Ferment

2022-02-02 · Makes about 1 quart

Ingredients

  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2-inch piece ginger, peeled and sliced
  • 1 stalk lemongrass, cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 2 orange habaneros, sliced into rounds
  • 2 red habaneros, sliced into rounds
  • 1 poblano, sliced into rounds
  • 2 serranos, sliced into rounds
  • 3 Indian long chiles, sliced into rounds
  • 1 red Fresno chile, sliced into rounds
  • 24 oz raw honey

Method

  1. Pack the garlic, ginger, lemongrass, and all the chiles into a clean quart mason jar.
  2. Pour the honey over the top and stir until everything is coated.
  3. Cover the jar with cheesecloth and set it at room temperature, out of direct sun.
  4. Let it ferment 2 to 4 weeks. Every day or so, stir, turn, or move the jar around so the solids stay coated in honey. Open it now and then to let gas out if you put a lid on it.
  5. It is ready when the honey has thinned and tastes of the chiles. Put a lid on it and store it at room temperature or in the fridge.

Notes

Use raw honey. Pasteurized honey has no live yeast, so it will not ferment. Water drawn out of the chiles thins the honey enough for the yeast to wake up. You will see small bubbles within a few days, and the honey turns runny.

Keep the solids coated. Moving the jar every day is what keeps mold away. Pieces that sit dry above the honey are where mold starts. If you see fuzzy mold, throw the batch out.

Honey is acidic, and fermentation makes it more so. If you have pH strips, the honey should read 4.6 or lower before you store it (moderate confidence on how fast it gets there with this much fresh chile).

The mix of chiles gives layers: fruity heat from the habaneros, grassy heat from the serranos, and body from the poblano. The lemongrass and ginger keep it bright. Use it on pizza, fried chicken, cheese, or in Spicy Honey Mustard.
