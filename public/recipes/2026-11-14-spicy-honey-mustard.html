Spicy Honey Mustard

2026-11-14 · Makes about 2½ cups

Ingredients

  • ½ cup brown mustard seed
  • ½ cup yellow mustard seed
  • ½ cup malt vinegar
  • ¼ cup rice wine vinegar
  • ½ cup white vinegar
  • 2 tsp kosher salt
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ¼ tsp ground allspice
  • ¼ tsp ground ginger
  • ¼ tsp ground cinnamon
  • ⅛ tsp ground nutmeg
  • ⅛ tsp ground clove
  • ½ tsp amchur (dried mango powder)
  • 5 Tbsp fermented chile honey
  • 4 Tbsp pickle juice

Method

  1. Combine the brown and yellow mustard seed with the malt, rice wine, and white vinegars in a jar. Cover and leave at room temperature for 2 days. The seeds soak up most of the liquid. Add a splash more vinegar if they look dry.
  2. Add the salt, turmeric, allspice, ginger, cinnamon, nutmeg, clove, amchur, chile honey, and pickle juice.
  3. Blend with an immersion blender or food processor until coarse and thick, 1 to 2 minutes. Add more pickle juice if it is too stiff to blend.
  4. Pack into a jar and refrigerate for 1 to 2 days before using. It keeps for months in the fridge.

Notes

This is the corrected version and it is not tested yet. The first batch used ½ tsp each of allspice, ginger, and cinnamon. That was too much. The warm spices took over. They are cut to ¼ tsp each here. The first batch also had ½ tsp lemon ketchup, which made it bitter and overpowering. It is gone.

Mustard heat comes from a reaction that starts when the ground or soaked seed meets liquid. Acid slows that reaction and fixes the heat level. Soaking in vinegar from the start gives a steady, moderate burn. Brown seed is hotter than yellow, so the even split keeps it spicy without going harsh.

Fresh mustard tastes bitter and sharp. A day or two in the fridge rounds it out. Taste it after the rest before you decide it needs more honey or salt.

The amchur adds a sour, fruity note that works with the chile honey. The pickle juice brings salt and acid and loosens the texture.
