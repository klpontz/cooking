Butter Pie Crust Dough

2006-10-08 · Makes one 9-inch crust

Ingredients

Standard Crust

  • 1¼ cups all-purpose flour
  • ½ Tbsp sugar
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 8 Tbsp (1 stick) cold unsalted butter, cut into ½-inch cubes
  • 3 Tbsp ice water, plus more as needed

Tall-Pie Crust (about 1⅓×, for deep, full pies)

  • 1⅔ cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tsp sugar
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • 10 Tbsp (1¼ sticks) cold unsalted butter, cut into ½-inch cubes
  • 4 Tbsp ice water, plus more as needed

Method

  1. Pick one list, standard or tall pie, and use only its amounts. Pulse the flour, sugar, and salt in a food processor to blend.
  2. Add the butter. Pulse in short bursts until the mixture looks like coarse meal, with some butter pieces still the size of small peas.
  3. Add the ice water. Pulse just until moist clumps form. Squeeze a big pinch in your fist. If it falls apart, add more water ½ Tbsp at a time and pulse again.
  4. Gather the dough into a ball and flatten it into a disk. Wrap it in plastic and chill at least 1 hour. It keeps in the fridge up to 2 days.
  5. Before rolling, let the dough soften a little at room temperature, until it bends at the edge without cracking.

Notes

Adapted from Bon Appétit, November 2003, via Epicurious. Printed 2006-10-08.

Tall pie: the second list is about 1⅓ times the standard recipe. A deep, full pie like the Pecan Cream Pie needs the extra dough for a tall, crimped edge. Double-crust pie: double the standard amounts, split the dough in half, and form two disks.

The food processor cuts the butter in fast, before your hands can warm it. The pea-size pieces of butter that remain turn to steam in the oven and push the dough into flaky layers.

Add water slowly. Too much water builds gluten, which makes the crust tough and makes it shrink in the pan. The hour in the fridge lets the flour hydrate evenly and lets the gluten relax.

The small amount of sugar is there to help the crust brown, not to sweeten it. That keeps this crust right for sweet pies and savory ones.
