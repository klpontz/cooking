Shallot and Guanciale Tart

2026-11-01 · Makes one 10-inch tart

Ingredients

  • ⅔ batch Walnut Tart Dough
  • All-purpose flour, for dusting
  • 1 tsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 3 oz guanciale, chopped
  • 3 cups thinly sliced shallots
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 egg yolk
  • ⅓ cup heavy cream
  • 2 oz Cowgirl Creamery Wagon Wheel, rind trimmed, shredded (about ½ cup)

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F. Take the dough out of the fridge and let it sit until it bends without cracking, 15 to 20 minutes.
  2. On a lightly floured counter, roll the dough to a 13-inch round. Roll it around the pin, lift it over a 10-inch tart pan with a removable bottom, and unroll it.
  3. Ease the dough into the corner where the base meets the sides. Do not stretch it. Press it up the sides and leave some above the rim. Pinch the rim into a tidy, even edge. Patch any cracks with scraps.
  4. Freeze the lined pan for 20 minutes. The butter must be firm when it goes in the oven.
  5. Line the dough with parchment and fill it to the rim with dried beans or pie weights. Pack the weights against the walls. Bake until the dough is set and the rim is light brown, about 10 minutes.
  6. Reduce the oven to 325°F. Lift out the parchment and weights. Return the shell to the oven and bake until the base looks dry and light gold, about 20 minutes. If the base domes, press it down with the back of a spoon. Cool the shell a little and leave the oven on.
  7. While the shell bakes, heat the olive oil in a large skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the guanciale. When it sizzles, reduce the heat and cook until the fat renders and the guanciale starts to brown, about 7 minutes. Do not let it crisp fully.
  8. Add the shallots and season generously with salt and pepper. Cook gently, stirring and scraping the pan often, until they are very soft, fragrant, and rich golden brown, about 30 minutes. Shallots can finish sooner than onions, so check at 20 minutes. Cool to room temperature.
  9. Whisk the egg yolk and cream in a bowl. Season lightly with salt and pepper. Fold in the cheese and the shallot mixture.
  10. Fill the shell and smooth the top. Bake until the filling is just set, about 20 minutes. Let the tart cool to warm before you cut it.

Notes

Adapted from the Onion and Pancetta Tart in Joshua McFadden with Martha Holmberg, Six Seasons: A New Way with Vegetables (Artisan, 2017), pp. 352–353. The dough is the book's Pecan Dough (p. 49) made with walnuts. See Walnut Tart Dough.

Unlike a quiche, this tart holds very little custard. One yolk and a third of a cup of cream only bind the filling. The shallots are the filling. This is why low crust walls still work, and why the tart reheats well the next day.

The swaps: guanciale for pancetta, shallots for yellow onion, and Wagon Wheel for Gruyère. Wagon Wheel is a pressed, washed-rind cow's milk cheese from Cowgirl Creamery. It melts like Gruyère and adds some funk. Guanciale renders more fat than pancetta, so the oil drops from the book's small glug to 1 teaspoon. Shallots are sweeter and cook a little faster than onions.

First attempt, 2026-11-01. I made the dough the day before and chilled it overnight. It cracked when I rolled it cold. The lined pan chilled only 5 minutes. After 10 minutes of blind baking with weights on, the walls had slumped and the base had domed.

Fixes for next time, not yet tested:

  • Let the dough sit out until it bends, 15 to 20 minutes after a long chill.
  • Handle it as little as you can. Warm butter softens the dough and the walls give.
  • Ease the dough into the pan. Stretched dough snaps back and shrinks.
  • Freeze the lined pan for 20 minutes. Five minutes in the fridge was not enough.
  • Fill the shell with weights to the rim and pack them against the walls. Weights in the center only hold the base.
  • Leave dough above the rim. It shrinks in the oven, so you lose a little height.
  • If the base domes when the weights come off, press it flat with the back of a spoon.
