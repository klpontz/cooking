Chimichurri

2026-11-14 · Makes about 1½ cups

Ingredients

  • ¼ cup dried oregano
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • ½ tsp red pepper flakes, more or less to taste
  • ½ tsp ground cumin
  • Kosher salt
  • ½ cup hot water
  • ¼ cup red wine vinegar
  • 8 medium garlic cloves
  • 2 Tbsp olive oil
  • ¼ cup fresh oregano leaves, finely minced
  • 1 tightly packed cup fresh parsley, finely minced
  • Black pepper

Method

  1. Combine the dried oregano, paprika, red pepper flakes, cumin, and a big pinch of salt in a bowl. Add the hot water and stir with a fork. Stir in the vinegar.
  2. Pound the garlic with a pinch of salt in a mortar and pestle to a rough paste. Drizzle in the olive oil and keep working the garlic and oil until they emulsify and no loose oil remains. Scrape the paste into the oregano mixture and stir.
  3. Stir in the fresh oregano and parsley.
  4. Let it sit at least 30 minutes at room temperature, or overnight in the fridge, so the dried oregano hydrates. Stir hard, taste, and season with salt and pepper.

Notes

Adapted from J. Kenji López-Alt, The New York Times.

Dried oregano carries the classic Argentine flavor, but it tastes dusty straight from the jar. Hot water wakes it up and softens it. The rest gives it time to fully hydrate.

Pounding the garlic with a little oil makes an emulsion that holds the garlic in suspension. The sauce tastes of garlic all the way through instead of in raw bites.

Use garden oregano for the fresh leaves. Spoon it over steak or anything off the fire, or over Pan-Roasted Chicken Thighs.
