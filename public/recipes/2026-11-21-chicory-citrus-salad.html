Chicory and Citrus Salad with Fennel

2026-11-21 · Serves 6 to 8

Ingredients

Pickled Shallots

  • 2 shallots, sliced into thin rings
  • 3 Tbsp white wine vinegar
  • Pinch of kosher salt

Salad

  • 1 head escarole
  • 1 small head radicchio
  • 1 small head frisée (optional)
  • 4 to 5 satsumas or 2 Cara Cara oranges
  • 1 fennel bulb, fronds saved
  • Small handful of tarragon or flat-leaf parsley leaves
  • Small bunch of chives, cut into 1-inch lengths
  • Flaky salt and black pepper, to finish

Dressing

  • 1 Tbsp shallot pickling vinegar (from the pickled shallots)
  • 2 Tbsp citrus juice, saved from cutting the fruit
  • Pinch of kosher salt
  • 5 Tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 Tbsp capers, rinsed (optional)

Method

  1. Pickled Shallots. Toss the shallots with the vinegar and salt in a small bowl. Let them pickle at least 20 minutes, stirring once.
  2. Salad. Tear the escarole, radicchio, and frisée into bite-size pieces. For a milder salad, soak the radicchio in ice water for 10 minutes. Wash and dry all the greens very well. Wet leaves water down the dressing.
  3. Peel the citrus. For oranges, cut away the peel and white pith with a knife. Slice into rounds over a bowl to catch the juice. Save 2 Tbsp of the juice.
  4. Halve the fennel, cut out the core, and slice it paper-thin, on a mandoline if you have one.
  5. Dressing. Stir 1 Tbsp of the shallot pickling vinegar, the citrus juice, and a pinch of salt in a small bowl. Whisk in the olive oil. Stir in the capers if using.
  6. Lift the shallots out of their vinegar. In a wide bowl, toss the greens, fennel, tarragon, and chives with most of the dressing. Taste and add salt if needed. Lay the citrus and pickled shallots on top and spoon over the rest of the dressing. Finish with flaky salt, black pepper, and a few fennel fronds. Serve right away.

Notes

Built for a rich table: crab with butter dip, rolls, and Pecan Cream Pie. What that meal lacks is bitterness, acid, and crunch. The chicories bring the bitterness, the citrus and vinegar bring the acid, and the fennel brings the crunch. There is no cheese and no nuts on purpose. The meal has plenty of both.

Citrus is a classic partner for crab. Fennel and tarragon both carry a faint anise note that ties the salad to the seafood.

Radicchio is the bitter one. The ice-water soak pulls out some of the bitterness and crisps the leaves. Salt and the sweet citrus also take the edge off. For kids, go heavier on escarole and citrus.

Shallots are milder and sweeter than red onion, and a quick pickle softens them further and turns them pink. The pickling vinegar picks up their flavor, so it does double duty as the acid in the dressing.

Do ahead: in the morning, pickle the shallots, cut the citrus, slice the fennel, and wash and dry the greens. Keep each part covered in the fridge. The shallots keep for days in their vinegar. Toss at the table, because chicories are sturdy, but dressed leaves still go limp in about 30 minutes.
