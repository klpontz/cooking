Pecan Cream Pie

2026-11-21 · Serves 8

Ingredients

Crust

  • 1 disk Butter Pie Crust Dough, made with the tall-pie amounts, chilled
  • All-purpose flour, for dusting

Pecan Layer and Topping

  • 2 large eggs
  • ⅔ cup (133 g) packed dark brown sugar
  • 1 tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt (or ½ tsp Morton)
  • 6 Tbsp unsalted butter
  • 2½ cups (300 g) raw pecans
  • 2 Tbsp sour cream
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract

Custard

  • 1½ cups heavy cream
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 6 large egg yolks
  • ¾ cup (150 g) packed dark brown sugar
  • ⅓ cup (40 g) cornstarch
  • ½ tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt (or ¼ tsp Morton)
  • ½ cup whole milk
  • 6 Tbsp cold unsalted butter, cut into pieces
  • 4 tsp vanilla extract

Whipped Topping

  • ¼ cup sour cream
  • 3 Tbsp powdered sugar
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • Pinch of kosher salt
  • 1 cup heavy cream

Method

  1. Crust. Make the Butter Pie Crust Dough with the tall-pie amounts. Chill it at least 1 hour, or up to 2 days.
  2. Set racks in the upper and lower thirds of the oven and heat to 425°F. Let the dough sit out until it bends at the edge without cracking, 5 to 10 minutes. Roll it on a lightly floured surface into a round ⅛ to ¼ inch thick. Roll it onto the pin and unroll it over a 9-inch metal pie pan. Lift the edges and let the dough slump into the pan. Do not stretch it. Trim the overhang to 1 inch, fold it under, and crimp so the edge sits up on the rim. Prick the bottom and sides with a fork. Freeze 20 minutes.
  3. Crumple 2 sheets of parchment, smooth them out, and lay them over the dough. Fill with pie weights or dried beans, packed up against the sides. Set the pan on a foil-lined baking sheet and bake on the lower rack until the edges are golden and the bottom looks opaque, 35 to 40 minutes. Tent the edges with foil if they brown too fast. Lift out the parchment and weights. Turn the oven down to 350°F.
  4. Pecan layer and topping. While the crust bakes, whisk the eggs, ⅔ cup brown sugar, and 1 tsp salt in a medium bowl. This egg mixture is for both the candied topping and the filling.
  5. Melt the 6 Tbsp butter in a medium saucepan over medium heat. Add the pecans and cook, stirring gently, until they darken a little and smell toasty, 5 to 7 minutes. Pour through a fine-mesh sieve set over a bowl. Keep both the pecans and the browned butter. Keep the pan for the custard. No need to wash it.
  6. Put ½ cup of the toasted pecans in a small bowl, add 1 Tbsp of the egg mixture, and toss. Spread on a parchment-lined sheet and bake on the upper rack until dry to the touch with a honeycomb look on the surface, about 5 minutes. Let cool.
  7. Finely chop the rest of the toasted pecans. Stir them into the remaining egg mixture with the browned butter, 2 Tbsp sour cream, and 1 tsp vanilla. Scrape into the crust and bake on the lower rack until mostly set with a slight jiggle in the center, 15 to 18 minutes. Cool on a rack until barely warm, at least 30 minutes.
  8. Custard. Heat the 1½ cups cream and 1 cup milk in the same saucepan over medium until steaming, about 2 minutes.
  9. Meanwhile, whisk the egg yolks, ¾ cup brown sugar, cornstarch, ½ tsp salt, and the remaining ½ cup milk in a medium bowl.
  10. Slowly pour the hot cream into the yolk mixture, whisking the whole time. Pour it all back into the pan. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until it bubbles, then turns glossy and thick like pudding, 3 to 4 minutes. Scrape into a bowl. Whisk in the 6 Tbsp cold butter and 4 tsp vanilla until the butter melts and the custard is smooth. Press plastic wrap onto the surface and let it stand while the pecan layer cools. Both can still be a little warm.
  11. Whisk the custard smooth and spread it over the pecan layer, all the way to the edges. Cover and chill at least 4 hours, up to 1 day.
  12. Topping. Whisk the ¼ cup sour cream, powdered sugar, 1 tsp vanilla, a pinch of salt, and the 1 cup cream to medium peaks. Spread it over the custard. Break the candied pecans into large pieces and scatter them on top just before serving.

Notes

Adapted from Joe Sevier, Bon Appétit, October 2025 (updated November 2026). Inspired by the Praline Pecan Pie at Das Dutchman Essenhaus in Middlebury, Indiana.

Spread it over two days. Day one: bake the crust and pecan layer, then store them covered at room temperature. The candied pecans keep 3 days airtight. Day two: make the custard and chill at least 4 hours, then whip the topping. Add the candied pecans at the last minute so they stay crunchy.

Use a 9-inch metal pan. Metal browns the bottom crust better than glass or ceramic, and it holds more. This is a tall pie, so the pan's capacity matters.

The sour cream does two jobs. In the pecan layer it keeps the sugar from crystallizing and takes the edge off the sweetness. In the topping its tang balances the sugar, and its thickness helps the whipped cream hold its shape.

Cook the custard until it actually bubbles. Cornstarch only thickens fully near a boil. Egg yolks also carry an enzyme that slowly thins a starch custard unless the heat kills it. A custard that never bubbled can set in the fridge and then weep. Chilling will not fix it.

The crust is our Butter Pie Crust Dough instead of the original cream cheese crust. The tall-pie amounts use the same 1⅔ cups of flour and 10 Tbsp butter as the original, so there is enough dough for a tall, crimped edge. It has almost no sugar, which balances a very sweet filling. Ease the dough into the pan without stretching it, freeze the lined pan, and pack the weights against the sides so the walls hold.
