Pan-Roasted Chicken Thighs

2014-06-02 · Serves 2 to 4

Ingredients

  • 6 skin-on, bone-in chicken thighs (about 2¼ lb)
  • Kosher salt
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 Tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 475°F.
  2. Pat the chicken dry. Season all over with salt and pepper.
  3. Heat the oil in a 12-inch cast-iron or heavy nonstick skillet over high heat until hot but not smoking.
  4. Lay the thighs in the pan skin side down and cook 2 minutes. Turn the heat to medium-high. Keep cooking skin side down until the fat renders and the skin is golden brown, about 12 minutes. Move the thighs around and turn the pan now and then so they brown evenly.
  5. Move the skillet to the oven and roast 13 minutes, still skin side down.
  6. Flip the thighs and roast until the skin is crisp and the meat is cooked through, about 5 minutes more.
  7. Move to a plate and rest 5 minutes before serving.

Notes

Adapted from the Bon Appétit Test Kitchen, Bon Appétit, June 2011.

The stovetop renders the fat and browns the skin through direct contact with hot iron. The oven then cooks the meat evenly from all sides. Leaving the thighs skin side down in the oven keeps the skin pressed to the hot pan the whole time.

Moving the thighs and turning the pan evens out hot spots on the burner. The pieces over the center brown first.

Thighs forgive overcooking. They are tender and juicy at 175°F to 185°F inside, well past the point where a breast would dry out. Serve with Chimichurri or Chili-Lime Nuoc Cham.
