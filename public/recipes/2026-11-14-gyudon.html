Gyudon

2026-11-14 · Serves 2

Ingredients

  • 12 oz (340 g) thinly shaved beef rib eye (shabu-shabu slices), frozen or fresh
  • ¾ cup (180 ml) dashi, homemade or Hondashi
  • 1 Tbsp (15 ml) shoyu, plus more to taste
  • 2 Tbsp (30 ml) dry sake
  • 2 Tbsp (30 ml) mirin
  • 1 Tbsp (15 g) sugar, plus more to taste
  • 1 large onion (about 6 oz / 170 g), slivered
  • 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
  • Kosher salt
  • 2–3 cups hot cooked rice
  • 2 poached large eggs (optional)
  • Sliced scallions
  • Beni-shoga (red pickled ginger)
  • Togarashi (ichimi or shichimi)

Method

  1. If the beef is frozen, thaw it fully. Seal it in a zip-top bag and submerge it in cold water for 15–20 minutes. Pat the slices dry and pull them apart.
  2. Bring a medium pot of water to a bare simmer, just short of a boil. In a small saucepan, combine the dashi, shoyu, sake, mirin, and sugar. Bring to a simmer over medium heat. Add the onion and simmer until half tender, about 5 minutes.
  3. While the onion simmers, blanch the beef. Swish a small handful of slices in the hot water for 10–15 seconds, until they just turn gray. Lift them into a strainer. Repeat with the rest. Skim the gray foam off the water between batches. Pour the water out.
  4. Add the blanched beef and ginger to the sauce. Simmer 1–2 minutes, turning the beef to coat, until the sauce is glossy and slightly reduced. Do not cook it longer — shaved rib eye toughens fast. Adjust with salt, sugar, or shoyu.
  5. Spoon the beef, onion, and sauce over bowls of hot rice. Top with a poached egg, scallions, beni-shoga, and togarashi. Serve immediately.

Notes

Adapted from a cookbook gyudon. The change is the blanch step, which keeps gray, sandy protein out of the sauce.

Where the grit comes from. The gray grains are coagulated meat protein, mostly myoglobin and other water-soluble proteins. Japanese cooks call it aku. It is harmless but looks like wet sand. Frozen beef makes much more of it. Ice crystals tear the muscle fibers, so the slices leak more juice as they thaw. If the beef goes into the sauce frozen, it drops the liquid below a simmer and thaws there. The juice then sets slowly as fine grains instead of clumping into foam. Stirring to break the frozen block apart crumbles those grains further. And ¾ cup of liquid in a wide skillet is too little to skim, so the protein dries onto the pan.

Why the blanch works. A 10–15 second dip in hot water, called shimofuri, sets that protein on the meat's surface. What leaks out stays in the water, and you pour it away. The sauce stays clear and glossy. The beef only needs a short finish in the sauce, so it stays tender. It is the same move as shabu-shabu.

If you skip the blanch. Thaw the beef fully first. Use a small saucepan or double the sauce, so there is enough liquid to skim. Add the beef in two batches so the simmer never stops. Skim the foam with a fine-mesh strainer as it rises.

Timing. The original recipe simmers the beef for 5 minutes. Shaved rib eye is cooked through in about 1 minute, and longer makes it tough and dry.
