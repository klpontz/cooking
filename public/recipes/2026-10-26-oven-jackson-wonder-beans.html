Oven Jackson Wonder Beans

2026-10-26 · Serves 6

Ingredients

  • 1 lb Rancho Gordo Jackson Wonder beans (one bag)
  • 1 heaping Tbsp bacon fat
  • ½ cup minced shallots
  • Ribs from 1 bunch collard greens (save the leaves for Sunday Collards)
  • 1 celery rib
  • 1 carrot
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 dried chiles
  • 1 tsp Rancho Gordo bean seasoning
  • 2 California bay leaves
  • Water
  • 2 dried shiitake mushrooms
  • 2 pieces kombu
  • Kosher salt and MSG

Method

  1. Soak the beans overnight in plenty of water. Drain. Heat the oven to 300°F.
  2. Melt the bacon fat in a Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the shallots and cook until soft, about 5 minutes.
  3. Strip the ribs from the collards and mince them small. Add them and cook until soft. Mince the celery small and add it. Do the same with the carrot. Prep each one while the last one cooks.
  4. Mince the garlic and add it. Add the chiles, bean seasoning, and bay leaves. Cook 1 minute.
  5. Add the beans and water to cover by 1 inch. Add the shiitake and kombu. Bring almost to a boil, cover, and move to the oven.
  6. After 30 minutes, pull out the kombu.
  7. Bake until the beans are tender, 1½–2 hours total. About halfway, season with salt and a pinch of MSG. If the beans peek above the liquid, add hot water.
  8. Pull out the chiles, bay leaves, and shiitake. Chop the shiitake and stir them back in. Season again with salt and MSG to taste.
  9. Let the pot rest off the heat 15 minutes. Serve warm.

Notes

Kevin's own pot, built around a bag of Jackson Wonder beans from the Rancho Gordo Bean Club. Jackson Wonder is an heirloom speckled lima. Rancho Gordo says even lima haters on their staff loved it and the rich broth it makes. Christmas limas or other large limas work too. Bigger beans may need more time.

The 300°F oven holds the pot just below a boil. The beans cook through gently, the skins stay whole, and the insides turn creamy.

Shiitake, kombu, and MSG build a deep savory broth with only a spoon of bacon fat. Pull the kombu at 30 minutes, before it turns slimy. The shiitake go back in, chopped, so nothing is wasted.

Salt twice. Salt halfway seasons the beans all the way through. Salt at the end corrects for the broth as it reduces.

Pair with Sunday Collards and Crispy Corn Bread. The collard leaves go in one pot and the ribs go in this one, so one bunch feeds both. Sop up the bean broth and the pot likker with the corn bread.
