Chile Verde Pork

2026-11-14 · Serves 8

Ingredients

  • 1 lb tomatillos, husked and rinsed
  • ⅔ lb poblano peppers
  • ½ lb Anaheim peppers
  • 2 jalapeño peppers
  • ½ lb white onion, quartered
  • 6 garlic cloves, unpeeled
  • Bell peppers (optional)
  • 4 lb boneless pork shoulder
  • 1 Tbsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 Tbsp whole cumin seed
  • 1 Tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 Tbsp fish sauce
  • Carrots, cut into large chunks (optional)
  • ½ to 1 cup cilantro, roughly chopped
  • Spinach, a few handfuls (optional)

Method

  1. Heat the broiler. Spread the tomatillos, poblanos, Anaheims, jalapeños, onion, garlic, and any bell peppers on a sheet pan. Broil until charred in spots and soft, 10 to 15 minutes, turning once. Peel the garlic. Stem the peppers and pull out the seeds if you want less heat.
  2. Cut the pork into very large chunks, about 3 inches. Season all over with the salt.
  3. Toast the cumin seed in a dry pan until fragrant, about 1 minute. Grind it.
  4. Heat the oil in the pressure cooker on Sauté. Brown the pork in batches so the pieces do not touch. Set each batch aside.
  5. Put all the pork back in the pot. Add the roasted vegetables and their juices, the cumin, the fish sauce, and any carrots. Scrape up the browned bits on the bottom.
  6. Cook on high pressure for 30 minutes. Let the pressure release naturally for 10 minutes, then release the rest.
  7. Lift out the pork. Add the cilantro and any spinach to the pot. Blend the sauce smooth with an immersion blender.
  8. Return the pork to the sauce. Taste and add salt.

Notes

The broiler does the work of a comal. Charred tomatillos and peppers give the sauce a smoky depth that boiled ones never have. Broil the garlic in its skin so it steams soft instead of burning.

Cut the pork big. Under pressure, small cubes overcook into dry strings before the fat and connective tissue break down. Big chunks stay juicy and pull apart with a fork.

There is no added liquid. The tomatillos give off plenty. Scrape the browned bits off the bottom before you lock the lid, or the pot can throw a burn warning.

The fish sauce does not taste of fish. It adds savory depth and a little salt, the same job an anchovy does in a pasta sauce. Taste before the final salt.

The extras stretch the batch. Spinach blended into the sauce makes it greener and milder. Carrots add a little sweetness. Serve with tortillas or Instant Pot Mexican Rice.
