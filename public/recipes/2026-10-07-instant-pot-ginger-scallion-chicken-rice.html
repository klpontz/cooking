Instant Pot Ginger-Scallion Chicken Rice

2026-10-07 · Serves 3

Ingredients

  • 3 boneless chicken breasts (about 1¾ lb)
  • 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine
  • 2 tsp light soy sauce
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • ½ tsp white pepper
  • 1¼ tsp kosher salt, divided
  • 2 cups jasmine rice
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil (or chicken fat)
  • 2-inch piece ginger: half sliced into coins, half finely grated (grated half is for the sauce)
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 bunch scallions, whites cut into 2-inch pieces, greens thinly sliced
  • 2 cups low-sodium chicken stock
  • 4 dried shiitake, crumbled (optional)
  • ⅓ cup neutral oil, for the sauce
  • Cucumber, cilantro, and chili crisp or sambal, to serve

Method

  1. Even out and marinate. Butterfly or pound the breasts to an even ¾ inch. Toss with the Shaoxing wine, soy sauce, sesame oil, white pepper, and ½ tsp salt. Let sit while you prep the rice.
  2. Rinse the rice in 3–4 changes of water until mostly clear. Drain well.
  3. Sauté. On Sauté, heat 2 tbsp oil. Add the ginger coins, garlic, and scallion whites and cook about 1 minute until fragrant. Add the rice and stir 1–2 minutes until glossy and slightly translucent at the edges.
  4. Deglaze. Turn Sauté off. Add the stock, shiitake, and ¾ tsp salt. Scrape the bottom completely clean to prevent a burn warning. Level the rice into an even layer. Do not stir again.
  5. Layer the chicken. Lay the breasts flat on the rice in one layer and pour the leftover marinade over the top. Keep the soy out of the bottom liquid.
  6. Pressure cook on High for 5 minutes. Let the pressure release naturally for 10 minutes, then vent the rest.
  7. Check the temperature. The thickest part should read 160°F. Carryover heat takes it to 165°F. If it reads low, close the lid on Keep Warm for 3–5 minutes. Move the chicken to a board and rest 5 minutes.
  8. Ginger-scallion oil. Put the grated ginger, scallion greens, and ½ tsp salt in a heatproof bowl. Heat ⅓ cup oil until shimmering, close to smoking, and pour it over. It should sizzle hard. A splash of soy or rice vinegar is optional.
  9. Finish. Fluff the rice and remove the ginger coins. Slice the chicken against the grain. Serve over the rice with the ginger-scallion oil, cucumber, cilantro, and chili crisp.

Notes

This is Hainanese chicken rice folded into one pot. The classic method poaches the chicken, then cooks the rice in the fat and broth. Here the rice cooks underneath and catches the chicken juices as they drip, so you get the same flavor in one step. The ginger-scallion oil carries as much of the dish as the chicken does.

Pounding the breasts to an even ¾ inch is what lets the chicken and rice finish together. Thick, uneven breasts need about 7 minutes, and the rice goes soft. Rinsing the rice and scraping the pot clean prevent gluey rice and the burn warning. Soy sugars scorch on the bottom, so the marinade stays on top of the chicken. Trust the thermometer, not the clock.

If you have chicken skin or schmaltz, render it in place of the oil in step 3. That is the traditional move and the biggest flavor upgrade. The dried shiitake add background savoriness without tasting of mushroom. A pinch of MSG in the rice works too. Serve the chili crisp on the side to keep the base mild for the whole family.
